## RACE — how racing generally works (20)

- [ ] 16. Cross-country (XC) races are usually raced over laps of a mixed climbing-and-descending course.
- [ ] 17. Downhill (DH) races are timed single runs down a hill — fastest time wins.
- [ ] 18. Enduro combines several timed downhill-focused stages linked by untimed transitions.
- [ ] 19. "DNF" is race shorthand for Did Not Finish.
- [ ] 20. Race categories are often split by age group and ability, so riders compete against similar peers.
- [ ] 21. A season-long series adds up points from several rounds to find an overall champion.
- [ ] 22. A "Fun" category lets newer or younger riders race without full competitive pressure.
- [ ] 23. Course marshals are volunteers stationed around the track to keep riders safe.
- [ ] 24. Riders often get to walk or ride the course before racing, to check the lines.
- [ ] 25. Race numbers (number boards) help marshals and timekeepers identify riders on course.
- [ ] 26. Mass-start races begin with every rider setting off together; time-trials start riders one at a time.
- [ ] 27. A "hole shot" is being first into the opening corner off the start line.
- [ ] 28. Neutral zones let riders regroup safely after a technical section before racing resumes.
- [ ] 29. Some races award a prize for the fastest lap of the day, separate from the overall result.
- [ ] 30. A "DQ" (disqualification) can happen for cutting the course or missing a checkpoint.
- [ ] 31. Feed zones on longer races let riders grab water or food without stopping.
- [ ] 32. Race briefings before the start explain the course, hazards and any special rules.
- [ ] 33. A "podium" is the top three finishers in a category, often called up for photos and prizes.
- [ ] 34. Some events use chip timing — a small tag on the bike that records your exact time automatically.
- [ ] 35. Junior races are often shortened versions of the adult course to suit younger riders.

## KNOW — bike mechanics & parts (12)

- [ ] 36. The "ABC" pre-ride check stands for Air, Brakes, Chain.
- [ ] 37. Knobbly tyres grip loose dirt far better than the smooth tyres on a road bike.
- [ ] 38. A dropper post lowers your saddle at the push of a lever, out of the way for descents.
- [ ] 39. Disc brakes squeeze pads onto a metal rotor near the wheel hub to slow you down.
- [ ] 40. Suspension front and rear helps the wheels stay in contact with rough ground.
- [ ] 41. Gears let you pedal easily up a hill and still pedal fast on the flat.
- [ ] 42. A quick-release skewer lets you take a wheel off without tools.
- [ ] 43. Flat pedals let your feet come off easily; clipless pedals click your shoes in.
- [ ] 44. A dry, squeaky chain wears out faster — regular oiling keeps it running smoothly.
- [ ] 45. Checking tyre pressure before a ride is one of the simplest ways to avoid a puncture.
- [ ] 46. Mountain bike wheels commonly come in 27.5-inch and 29-inch sizes.
- [ ] 47. A hardtail has front suspension only; a full-suspension bike has shocks front and rear too.

## KNOW — riding technique & skills (15)

- [ ] 48. Looking ahead down the trail, not at your front wheel, helps you steer smoothly.
- [ ] 49. Standing up on the pedals over bumps lets your knees and elbows absorb the shock.
- [ ] 50. A "manual" is lifting the front wheel without pedalling, using body weight alone.
- [ ] 51. A "bunny hop" lifts both wheels off the ground at once to clear an obstacle.
- [ ] 52. Leaning the bike into a corner (not just your body) helps the tyres grip.
- [ ] 53. Braking mostly with the back brake and gently with the front keeps you balanced.
- [ ] 54. "Pumping" — pushing down on the bike over rollers — can build speed without pedalling.
- [ ] 55. Keeping pedals level through a corner stops the inside one clipping the ground.
- [ ] 56. A wide, ready body position — elbows out, knees bent — makes a bike easier to control.
- [ ] 57. Practising slow, controlled balance (like a track stand) is real bike skill, not just speed.
- [ ] 58. Shifting to an easier gear before a climb is smoother than shifting once you're already struggling.
- [ ] 59. Trusting the bike to roll over small bumps, rather than tensing up, keeps you smoother.
- [ ] 60. A "wheelie" is riding on the back wheel alone, front wheel in the air.
- [ ] 61. Cornering with your eyes up and looking through the turn helps you commit to the line.
- [ ] 62. Two fingers covering the brake levers is usually plenty — no need to death-grip the bars.

## KNOW — safety & trail etiquette (13)

- [ ] 63. A properly fitted helmet should sit level, snug, and not tipped back off your forehead.
- [ ] 64. Give way to walkers and horse riders on shared trails.
- [ ] 65. Horses can spook easily, so slow right down and pass wide and calm.
- [ ] 66. A friendly "hello" as you pass other trail users is good manners and lets them know you're there.
- [ ] 67. Take your litter home so the trails stay great for everyone.
- [ ] 68. On narrow trails, riders heading uphill usually have priority over those coming down.
- [ ] 69. Always let someone know roughly where you're riding and when you'll be back.
- [ ] 70. Riding with others is safer than riding alone on trails you don't know well.
- [ ] 71. A gate you find on the trail should be left exactly as you found it — open or shut.
- [ ] 72. Wet roots and rocks are much more slippery than dry ones.
- [ ] 73. Wearing gloves helps protect your hands if you do come off.
- [ ] 74. A basic first-aid kit and phone are worth carrying on longer rides.
- [ ] 75. Riding within your own ability, not just following faster friends, keeps rides fun and safe.

- [ ] 78. The UCI (Union Cycliste Internationale) is mountain biking's world governing body.
- [ ] 79. "Singletrack" means a trail only wide enough for one rider at a time.
- [ ] 80. A trail centre is a purpose-built network of mountain bike trails, often graded by difficulty.
- [ ] 81. Trail difficulty is often colour-graded, a bit like ski slopes — green, blue, red, black.
- [ ] 82. E-bikes use a pedal-assist motor to help on climbs, but you still have to pedal.
- [ ] 83. Freeride and slopestyle are MTB disciplines built around jumps and tricks rather than racing.
- [ ] 84. Fat bikes have extra-wide tyres designed to ride over sand or snow.

## KNOW — trail features & terrain reading (10)

- [ ] 86. A "rock garden" is a rough, technical section covered in rocks and roots.
- [ ] 87. A "drop" is where the trail steps down suddenly and the rider becomes briefly airborne.
- [ ] 88. "Flow trails" use rollers and berms so momentum carries you through with less braking.
- [ ] 89. A "berm" is a banked corner that lets riders carry speed round a bend.
- [ ] 90. A "gap jump" clears a hole in the trail, not just a step down.
- [ ] 91. Contour lines on a map show hills — lines close together mean a steep climb.
- [ ] 92. Reading the trail ahead for line choice is one of the most important MTB skills.
- [ ] 93. A "switchback" is a sharp zig-zag turn used to gain height on a steep slope.
- [ ] 94. Loose gravel over hardpack ("marbles") can be slippier than mud in the wrong spot.
- [ ] 95. Off-camber sections slope sideways across the trail, testing your balance and line choice.

- [ ] 96. Weather-appropriate layers you can add or remove make a big difference on longer rides.
- [ ] 97. Carrying water (or a hydration pack) matters even on cooler days.
- [ ] 98. A spare inner tube or repair kit means a puncture doesn't end your ride early.
- [ ] 99. Sunglasses or clear glasses protect your eyes from branches, grit and glare.
- [ ] 100. A quick pre-ride bike check takes two minutes and can save a much longer walk home.
