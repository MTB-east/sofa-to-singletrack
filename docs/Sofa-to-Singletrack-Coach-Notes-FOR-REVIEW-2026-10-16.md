## 4. Coach tips (the 'what riders worry about' section)

**Nervous on descents**

Look through the corner or the section ahead, not down at your front wheel. Where your eyes go, the bike tends to follow. Start slower than feels necessary; speed comes with confidence, not the other way round.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Aches and niggles**

A bit of new-activity discomfort in the first couple of weeks isn't unusual, but anything sharp, one-sided, or that doesn't ease off deserves a proper check from a physio or GP rather than pushing through it.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Struggling on climbs**

Everyone's got a different engine. The point is to ride your own pace and get to the top however long it takes. Sit slightly further back and keep pedalling smoothly rather than mashing the gear.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Missed sessions or lost motivation**

This happens to everyone at some point. Don't try to catch up on missed sessions. Just get back on for the next one on the plan and let the momentum build again naturally.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**The bike feels wrong**

Most early wobbles come down to saddle height (should let your leg almost straighten at the bottom of the pedal stroke) or tyre pressure (softer than road bikes, but not so soft it feels vague). Worth a quick check before assuming it's you.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Bad weather**

Riding in the wet or wind is a genuinely useful skill to build, but if it's unsafe (ice, high winds, thunder), swap to the indoor/trainer option for that session rather than risk it.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Comparing yourself with other riders**

Everyone on this plan started from a different point. The only comparison that matters is you against where you were a few weeks ago.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

## 5. Race week (adults)

**Taper week**

Feeling like you haven't done enough this week is completely normal. The whole point of a taper is doing less, so your legs are fresh. Trust the rest.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**The day before**

Get your kit and bike sorted the night before so the morning is calm. Eat a meal you know agrees with you, and try to get a decent night's sleep. Nerves are normal and won't stop you riding well.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Race morning**

Nerves before a first race are completely normal, even for people who've raced for years. Remember the goal today is finishing your own race, at your own pace. Enjoy it.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Welcome**

Welcome, [rider's name]'s grown-up. The first game, [first session], is short on purpose: fifteen minutes, somewhere flat, and you count out loud. Helmet on before the bike moves, and stay within sight the whole time. When it is done, tick it here and [rider's name] collects the first badge.

*The grown-up answers for the child after each game. Nothing medical, nothing about pushing harder.*

**Grown-up answered "Loved it"**

That is the whole plan working. Nothing to change: same place, next game, and let them tell someone about the badge.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Grown-up answered "Fine"**

Fine is good. Fine means it was not scary and not boring. The next game adds one small thing, so keep the pace and the place the same.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Grown-up answered "Found it hard"**

Play the same game again before moving on, somewhere flatter if you can, and stop while they are still enjoying it. The badge is for trying, not for perfect. If it was the standing or the braking that was hard, lower the saddle a touch.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Grown-up answered "Had to stop early"**

Stopping early is fine and you made the right call. Next time out, halve the number of goes and finish with something they already like doing. A short, happy session beats a long one every time.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Grown-up answered "Didn't get to it this week"**

Nothing lost. The game is still there and so is the badge. Pick a day this week, keep it short, and do the same game rather than skipping ahead.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Race week**

Race week for a Junior is a normal week with shorter games. Nothing new to learn now: a lap of the park, a whistle stop or two, and plenty of talking about what race day looks like, so nothing is a surprise.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**The day before**

Bike checked together tonight, helmet and clothes laid out, a snack packed. Tell them the plan for the morning in order: park, sign on, look at the course, line up, ride, cake. Early night for everyone.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Race morning**

Fun race means fun race. Get there with time to walk a bit of the course together, remind them where you will be standing, and that finishing is the whole job. Take the photo at the finish, not the start.

Crew: ☐ keep  ☐ change  ☐ drop

**Graduation**

Sixteen games, sixteen badges, and a child who rides a trail standing up. A fun race is next if they want it, and the whole plan works again at the next age up: same games, bigger child, longer laps.

## 7. House style the notes were written to

Always: Keep it plain, practical and specific: real advice you'd say trackside, not generic hype. End on something forward-looking (the next session) instead of a pat on the back.

Avoid: No exclamation-mark enthusiasm, no fitness-app clichés ("crush it", "you've got this"), no medical claims.
